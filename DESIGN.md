# Design

Match the existing Nematic game and DNA damage lab on esmirnov.ai.
Use the same sage, charcoal, and clay palette.
Use Arial for prose and Courier for compact controls.
Keep the component and canvas transparent.

Use one large sample with 12 visible, independent factor sliders.
Keep all sliders in the document flow.
Use native controls with visible focus and 44-pixel targets.
Use thin rules and plain sections.

Keep live defects large enough to distinguish in the sample.
Use a nonmodal detail popup anchored to the selected cell.
Keep the enlarged anatomy view optional.
Keep Explore visible beside Pause and Reset sample.
Use normal clicks or taps without a context menu.
Use normal document flow without pinned panels or inner scroll containers.

On narrow embeds, put the controls below the sample.
Use container queries for the component layout.
Permit native vertical touch scrolling and pinch zoom over the canvas.

Use flexible tails and short trails to explain motion.
Draw the selected head, tail, and midpiece shapes explicitly.
Draw separate heads and tails when the cell has more than one.
Draw no tail when the cell has an absent-tail trait.

Show midpiece damage separately from head, tail, and DNA differences.
Mouse currents change physical movement only.
Factor controls prepare a new synthetic population with the selected combination.
Use `--sl-damage: #b82735` for DNA damage and defect accents.

Keep red DNA fragments inside a compact nucleus.
Keep typical heads compact, typical midpieces short and thin, and tails long and tapered.
Use no external cross marks, loose DNA helices, or banded midpiece barrels.

Start paused for reduced-motion visitors.
Stop automatic motion in hidden tabs and outside the viewport.
Cap pixel density and motion time steps.
Keep all catalog categories and their explanations available as text.
Identify the catalog as selected teaching examples rather than a complete clinical classification.

## Cell details

Keep the popup near its cell as the cell moves.
Keep the popup inside the sample area when possible.
Show movement, vitality, head, tail, midpiece, and DNA traits.
Use the same red damage color as the live cell.
Keep sample controls available while the popup is open.

Show Concentration with a number and the unit `cells / model field`.
Keep the count near the live sample.
Use synthetic density without clinical thresholds.
